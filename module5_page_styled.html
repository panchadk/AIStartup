Pick a product idea where a camera helps (sorting recyclables, plant diseases, product defects, food types, parts), and photograph 3 or more classes with at least 50 images each. Vary backgrounds, lighting and angles, and avoid duplicates. Put one folder per class inside a folder named my_data, zip it as my_data.zip, and upload it in Colab. No photos yet? The notebook can build a small demo dataset from CIFAR-10 so you can start straight away.

Notes (about 10 minutes)

  • A convolutional neural network (CNN) learns local visual patterns: edges first, then textures, then object parts.
  • Transfer learning starts from a model pretrained on a large dataset and adapts it. It needs far less data and compute than training from scratch.
  • Two common approaches: freeze the backbone and train only a new head, or unfreeze and fine-tune everything at a low learning rate. A good recipe does the first, then the second.
  • Data augmentation (flips, crops, colour jitter) adds variety and reduces overfitting on small datasets. Apply it to training images only.
  • Use a confusion matrix, not just accuracy, to see which classes get mixed up.
  • Small test sets are noisy: with about 25 test images, one mistake moves the accuracy by about 4 points.

Exercises (all in the notebook)

  1. Collect and check your dataset. At least 3 classes, 50 or more images each, no duplicates or unreadable files.
  2. Split it. Write a stratified split so each class is divided in the same proportions.
  3. Transforms. Build a training pipeline with augmentation and an evaluation pipeline without it, both normalized like ImageNet.
  4. Baseline. Train a ResNet-18 from scratch on your photos and record its test accuracy.
  5. Transfer learning. Load a pretrained ResNet-18, replace the head, and freeze the backbone.
  6. Two phases. Train the head, then unfreeze and fine-tune everything at a lower learning rate.
  7. Confusion matrix and worst mistakes. Write the confusion matrix, then review the 10 most confident wrong predictions and write down 3 patterns.
  8. Compare from scratch, head only, and fine-tuned, and write your conclusion.
  9. Stretch: compare two backbones on accuracy versus speed.
