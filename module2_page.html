Notes (about 10 minutes)

  • A layer computes a weighted sum of its inputs plus a bias, then applies a nonlinearity such as ReLU or sigmoid.
  • Without nonlinearities, stacked layers collapse into a single linear function, however many layers you add.
  • Backpropagation applies the chain rule layer by layer, from the loss back to every weight. It tells you how much each weight contributed to the error.
  • Gradient descent moves each weight a small step against its gradient. The step size is the learning rate.
  • Gradient checking compares your hand-derived gradients with numerical gradients (nudge a weight up and down and watch the loss) or with autograd. It is the standard way to catch backprop bugs.
  • Softmax turns class scores into probabilities. Subtract the largest score first, or large numbers overflow.

The network you will build

z1     = X @ W1 + b1          # hidden pre-activation
h      = max(0, z1)           # ReLU
logits = h @ W2 + b2          # class scores
loss   = mean cross-entropy of softmax(logits)

Exercises (all in the notebook)

  1. Look at the data. Three interleaved spirals. Explain why a straight line cannot separate them.
  2. Forward pass. Write forward(). Check: output shape and ReLU are correct.
  3. Softmax and loss. Write softmax_cross_entropy(). Check: equal scores give a loss of log(3), and huge scores do not give nan.
  4. Backpropagation. Write backward() by hand. Check: all four gradients match numerical gradients to better than 1e-6.
  5. Train. Write the gradient descent loop. Check: loss falls and accuracy is 95% or higher.
  6. Beat a linear model. Compare with a linear classifier and view your decision regions.
  7. Compare with PyTorch. Your gradients should match autograd.
  8. Stretch: add momentum and compare how fast the loss falls.
