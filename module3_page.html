Module 3: Build your own training loop

Project: write a reusable training and validation loop, make a model overfit on purpose, fix it, and track experiments with a 10-run sweep. Time: about 8 hours core, plus about 4 hours for the stretch challenge.

Done when: your fit() function trains, validates, saves the best checkpoint and can stop early; your regularized model beats the overfit baseline on validation data; and you can explain three changes that improved your validation score.

Notes (about 10 minutes)

  • Split your data three ways. Train on the training set, tune on the validation set, and use the test set once at the end.
  • Overfitting means low training loss but high validation loss. Fight it with more data, augmentation, dropout, weight decay, or early stopping.
  • Early stopping ends training when validation performance stops improving. Keep the best checkpoint, not the last one.
  • Adam is a strong default optimizer. The learning rate is usually the most important hyperparameter.
  • Log the settings and metrics of every run with an experiment tracker such as Weights & Biases or MLflow, so runs can be compared and reproduced.
  • Set random seeds, and change one thing at a time, so you know what helped.

Exercises (all in the notebook)

  1. Setup. Load a small training set (2,000 images), a validation set, and the test set.
  2. Evaluate. Write evaluate(). Check: it matches a hand-computed loss, even with uneven batches.
  3. Train one epoch. Write train_one_epoch(). Check: loss goes down on an easy problem.
  4. The full loop. Write fit() with validation, checkpointing, early stopping, logging and a scheduler hook. Check: six tests on a toy problem.
  5. Overfit on purpose. Train a big model on 2,000 images and watch the validation loss turn upward.
  6. Fix it. Apply at least two of dropout, weight decay, and early stopping. Check: smaller gap or lower validation loss.
  7. Track experiments. Write run_experiment(), with optional Weights & Biases logging.
  8. Sweep. Run 10 settings (5 learning rates x 2 batch sizes) and summarize which won and why.
  9. Final exam. Score your best model on the test set, once.
  10. Stretch: add a learning rate scheduler and compare.
