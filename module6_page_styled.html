Notes (about 10 minutes)

  • An embedding is a vector (a list of numbers) that represents the meaning of a text. Similar meanings give similar vectors.
  • Cosine similarity compares two vectors by the angle between them. It is the standard way to rank search results.
  • Attention lets a model weigh which other words matter for each word. Transformers are built from attention layers, and they produce the embeddings.
  • Long documents are split into chunks. Chunk size affects both relevance and cost.
  • Keyword search finds exact words; semantic search finds related meaning. Combining both often works best.
  • Always measure a search engine with a test set of queries, not by trying a few examples.

Exercises (all in the notebook)

  1. Load your documents. At least 6,000 words in plain text files.
  2. Chunk them. Write chunk_text() to pack whole paragraphs into chunks of limited size.
  3. Embed. Load a Hugging Face sentence-embedding model and write embed(). Embed 100 or more chunks.
  4. Cosine similarity. Write cosine_scores() yourself.
  5. Top matches. Write top_k(), then try the search on questions of your own.
  6. Measure. Write 20 test queries with gold phrases and write evaluate_search(). Target: at least 15 of 20 in the top 3.
  7. Compare chunk sizes fairly (with the same text budget) and choose the best one.
  8. Stretch: add a re-ranking step and measure the gain.
